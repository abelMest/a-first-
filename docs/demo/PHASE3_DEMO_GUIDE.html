Phase 3 demo guide: READINESS

Local instance (http://localhost:8710), course Data Science & Analytics for Public Health. About 15 minutes. Screenshots taken 20 November 2026. The scenario is a draft awaiting expert sign-off, and every learner shown is a scripted test account (evidence class: dev).

Before you start

  • Docker Desktop running; http://localhost:8710 opens.
  • Reset the demo (about 1 minute; repeat after any rehearsal): cd /c/AFIRST_Backup/afirst-rebuild && bash dev/demo_phase3.sh
  • Two windows: teacher1 (or admin) on Reports → Readiness, and scenario (student password in dev/.env) on the course.

In rehearsal, not on stage: log in once as scenario to dismiss the "Find your way around" tour, then run the reset script again. The scenario allows one attempt; the reset gives it back.

1. The idea (2 minutes)

Phase 1 answers questions; Phase 2 adapts the path; Phase 3 asks whether the learner can do the work. Knowledge Checks show what a learner knows. READINESS adds a realistic outbreak scenario in which they make the decisions a district data analyst makes, with consequences. The result combines the scenario, the Knowledge Checks and, later, a supervisor's rating.

It answers the advisory point from 6 November on moving from certificates to capability, and it is built the same way as Phases 1 and 2: authored content, a standard Moodle Lesson, a small report. The AI does not invent the scenario and does not grade the learner.

2. The learner's view

1

An early signal

Window: scenario · Final Course Activities → Readiness scenario

The scenario opens with a radio report and a rise in surveillance counts. Three options: verify, declare at once, or wait.

Every option is plausible. The learner chooses what they would actually do at work.

2

Immediate consequence

Choose "Treat it as a signal to verify", then Submit

Each choice is followed by feedback explaining its consequence, written by people, not by AI.

The feedback teaches as it assesses: event-based signals are early warnings that must be verified.

3

A critical choice

Decisions 2 and 3: the line list, then a request for the data

Decision 2 tests reproducible cleaning; a poor choice branches to an extra step where the numbers no longer reconcile. Decision 3 is data governance: the journalist, the NGO and the messaging group.

Posting the identifiable line list in a large group is marked critical: it caps the result at "not yet independent", however well the rest goes.

4

Analysis and communication

Decisions 4 to 7

Attack rates and risk ratio, the epidemic curve, choosing the analysis, and finally the situation report for the coordination meeting.

Each decision scores the domains it tests: signals, data quality, governance, analysis, communication.

5

End of the scenario

After decision 7

The learner sees the Lesson score: 24 out of 24 on the best path.

Today the learner sees only a score. The next development step is to tell them their readiness result and what is missing, as Phase 2 now does for unlocks.

3. The teacher's view

6

The readiness report

Window: teacher1 · Reports → Readiness (refresh after the live play)

One row per learner: the readiness result, a score per domain, the Knowledge Checks, the supervisor rating (when configured), and what is missing. The thresholds are written at the top.

Five results: ready for independent work, ready with supervision, not yet ready, in progress, not started. The live player now shows "Ready for independent work".

7

Why a learner is not ready

Scroll the report

Student2 did everything well except share identifiable data: Not yet ready, Critical choice. Student3 shows every gap; Student4 is ready with supervision, with the three gaps named.

The report never just says "fail": it names what is missing, so the supervisor knows what to work on.

8

Authored, scored, traceable

Open the Lesson → Edit (teacher)

Every option has its feedback, a score and the next decision. The scenario lives in one file in the repository (courses/ds4h/readiness/scenarios/DS4H-RDY-01.json) and is built into Moodle by a script, so an expert reviews and versions it like any course content.

Nothing here is generated. The expert signs off the content and the scores before any learner sees it.
