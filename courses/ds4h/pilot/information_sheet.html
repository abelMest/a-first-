A-FIRST pilot study: information for course participants

You are invited to take part in a study of A-FIRST, a set of learning supports added to this course by Africa CDC. Please read this page before you decide. Taking part is voluntary.

What is being studied

A-FIRST adds two things to the course:

  • a course assistant ("Ask the course") that answers questions only from the approved course material and shows where each answer comes from;
  • adaptive activities: optional module tests that let you move faster through topics you already know, and short review units with a retake when a Knowledge Check shows a gap.

The study asks whether these supports help participants understand the course content, and how participants experience them.

What taking part involves

  1. A short starting questionnaire (about 3 minutes) and a baseline test of 18 questions (about 15 minutes), before you use the course assistant.
  2. Following the course as usual over about three months, using the assistant and review units as much or as little as you like.
  3. A questionnaire in the middle and at the end of the pilot (about 5 minutes each).
  4. An endline test with the same 18 questions at the end of the pilot.
  5. Some participants (6 to 8) will be invited to an optional interview of about 45 minutes. You can say no to the interview and still take part in the rest.

The tests and questionnaires do not count towards your course grade or certificate.

About the course assistant

Answers from the course assistant are generated by AI and grounded in approved course content. Verify operational decisions with a qualified supervisor. If an answer looks wrong, use "Report a problem with this answer" under it to send it for human review; reports are reviewed within 48 hours.

What information is collected

  • Your answers to the tests and questionnaires.
  • Your course activity in Moodle: activities opened, Knowledge Check and module test results, and which review units you were offered.
  • The questions you ask the course assistant, the answers it gives and the sources it cites.
  • How you usually connect to the internet and which device you use, to check that the supports work for everyone.

How your information is protected

  • All data stay on Africa CDC servers. The course assistant runs on an Africa CDC server; your questions are not sent to any outside AI company.
  • Before analysis your name is replaced by a study code. The key linking codes to names is held only by [host site supervisor name], under controlled access.
  • Results are reported only for groups, never for individuals. Quotes from questionnaires or interviews are reported without names or details that could identify you.
  • Study data are kept for [retention period] and then deleted.

Your choices

  • Taking part is voluntary. If you do not take part, you keep full access to the course and all its activities.
  • You can withdraw at any time without giving a reason, by changing your answer in the consent activity to "No" or by writing to the contact below. Data collected up to that point will be [kept / deleted on request — to be set by the ethics committee].

Possible risks and benefits

The main cost is the time for the tests and questionnaires (about 45 minutes over three months). The course assistant may sometimes give an incomplete answer; it is designed to say when the course does not cover a question, and every answer shows its source so you can check it. You may benefit from quicker answers and targeted review; the study will help Africa CDC decide whether to offer these supports more widely.
